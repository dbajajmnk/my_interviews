# 1. Module Objective

This module prepares you to understand and defend the **AI & Data Campus curriculum as a senior technical trainer, architect, mentor, and curriculum owner**.

The interview objective is **not** to claim that every line in the workbook is perfect.

The objective is to demonstrate that you can:

1. Understand the intent of the curriculum.
2. Identify its strengths.
3. Identify execution risks without sounding negative.
4. Explain how the curriculum should be delivered.
5. Connect learning objectives to hands-on outcomes.
6. Defend sequencing and architecture decisions.
7. Recommend improvements with business justification.
8. Handle curriculum cross-questioning.
9. Position yourself as someone who can **own delivery**, not merely teach slides.
10. Maintain evidence-first, no-bluff answers.

# 12. Mixed-Skill Audience Strategy

## Interview Question

**"How would you handle a batch where learners have different skill levels?"**

### Strong Answer Structure

1. Baseline diagnostic.
2. Define non-negotiable core outcomes.
3. Provide foundation material for weak learners.
4. Provide stretch tasks for advanced learners.
5. Use guided labs before independent labs.
6. Establish mentor checkpoints.
7. Monitor weekly assessment performance.
8. Run targeted remediation.
9. Preserve the common capstone outcome.

### Answer

> "I would avoid creating two separate classes inside one classroom. I would establish a common minimum outcome, then differentiate the depth of practice. Learners who need support receive guided exercises and remediation, while stronger learners receive extension tasks such as optimization, security or architecture challenges."

# 15. Capstone Design Standard

A good capstone should test more than whether the application runs.

## Capstone Rubric

### 1. Problem Definition
Is the business problem clear?

### 2. Architecture
Is the architecture appropriate?

### 3. Implementation
Does the system function?

### 4. Data
Is ingestion/retrieval handled correctly?

### 5. Security
Are secrets/access controlled?

### 6. Reliability
Does it handle errors?

### 7. Testing
Are critical paths tested?

### 8. Observability
Can failures be diagnosed?

### 9. Cost / Performance
Has the learner considered efficiency?

### 10. Documentation
Can somebody else operate it?

### 11. Presentation
Can the learner explain the business and technical outcome?

### Answer

# 18. Q3 — Is 8 Weeks Enough?

Do not say simply yes/no.

### Strong Answer

> "Eight weeks can be sufficient for a focused engineering bootcamp if prerequisites are clear and the target proficiency is defined. The more important question is what capability the learner must demonstrate by Week 8. If the target is production-ready independent mastery of every technology listed, that would be unrealistic. If the target is strong foundation plus hands-on integration and a defensible capstone, the structure can work."

This answer demonstrates scope management.

```text

        ↓
State What You Know
        ↓
Connect to Known Architecture
        ↓

        ↓

In a solution I would evaluate it based on X, Y and Z, and verify service-specific limits against the current documentation."
